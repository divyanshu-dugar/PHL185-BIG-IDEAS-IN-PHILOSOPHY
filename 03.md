## 1. The Sophists: Persuasion Over Truth

Before understanding Socrates, we must understand **the Sophists**, because Socrates defined himself *against* them.

### Who Were the Sophists?

* Professional **teachers and diplomats** in ancient Athens
* Traveled from city to city
* **Charged fees** to teach rhetoric and persuasion
* Taught skills useful for success in politics and public life

### Core Sophist Beliefs

* There is **no absolute truth**
* Truth varies:

  * From culture to culture
  * From time to time
  * From individual to individual
* **Knowledge cannot be clearly distinguished from opinion**

### Their Goal

* Not to discover truth
* But to **win arguments**
* If truth is relative, then persuasion is more important than correctness

> For the Sophists, success meant convincing others — not being right.

---

## 2. Skepticism vs Sophism

* **Sophism**: Uses skepticism about truth to justify persuasion
* **Skepticism**: Questions knowledge but does not necessarily reject truth

Socrates accepts **intellectual humility**, but rejects the Sophists’ conclusion that truth does not matter.

---

## 3. Socrates and Plato

### Socrates

* Did **not write anything**
* Believed philosophy should be lived, not written
* Known only through others’ writings

### Plato

* Student of Socrates
* Deeply influenced by him
* Wrote philosophical works in the form of **dialogues** (like plays)
* In most dialogues, **Socrates is the main character**

---

## 4. Plato’s Dialogues: Three Periods

### 1. Early Dialogues

Examples:

* *Euthyphro*
* *Crito*
* *Apology*

Characteristics:

* Reflect **Socrates’ own views**
* Focus on ethical questions
* Use the **Socratic Method**
* Often end without a final answer

---

### 2. Middle Dialogues

Examples:

* *Meno*
* *Phaedo*
* *Symposium*
* *Republic*

Characteristics:

* Plato begins expressing **his own philosophy**
* Still presented through Socrates
* Introduce deeper metaphysical ideas

---

### 3. Late Dialogues

Example:

* *Laws*

Characteristics:

* Address **problems and limitations** of earlier theories
* More technical and complex
* Socrates’ role is reduced
* Plato increasingly speaks in his own voice

---

## 5. The Socratic Method

### Education as Discovery, Not Transmission

* Socrates believed **knowledge already exists within the soul**
* The role of education is to **draw it out**, not insert it

> Socrates compared himself to a **midwife** — helping ideas be born, not creating them.

### The Role of the Teacher

* Not to lecture
* Not to give answers
* But to help students **discover truth for themselves**

---

## 6. Irony and Questioning

The Socratic Method begins with **irony**:

* Socrates pretends to be ignorant
* He asks others to teach him
* Through questioning, he reveals that they do not truly understand what they claim to know

This exposes **false confidence** and intellectual arrogance.

---

## 7. Dialectic: Progress Through Questioning

Dialectic is the structured conversation Socrates uses to approach truth.

### How Dialectic Works

1. Socrates asks a question (e.g., *What is justice?* *What is piety?*)
2. Someone proposes an answer
3. Socrates asks follow-up questions
4. Contradictions or problems appear
5. The answer is shown to be inadequate

This process:

* Eliminates false answers
* Moves closer to truth
* Shows that **knowing requires examination**

> Progress is made by discovering what we do **not** know.

---

## 8. Socrates vs the Sophists

* Sophists claim to know and teach wisdom
* Socrates shows they **know nothing**
* Sophists seek victory
* Socrates seeks truth

This made Socrates deeply unpopular.

---

## 9. The Oracle of Delphi and Socratic Wisdom

### The Story

* Socrates’ friend **Chaerephon** asked the Oracle of Delphi:

  > “Is anyone wiser than Socrates?”
* The Oracle replied: **No**

### Socrates’ Investigation

* Socrates was confused by the answer
* He questioned politicians, poets, and craftsmen
* He discovered:

  * They believed they were wise
  * But could not justify their knowledge

### Socrates’ Conclusion

> Socrates is wiser only because **he knows that he is not wise**.

True wisdom = **awareness of ignorance**.

---

## 10. Socrates’ View of Virtue (Arete)

### Virtue as Human Excellence

* **Arete** means excellence or virtue
* To live well, humans must achieve excellence in being human

### Virtue Is Knowledge

* Virtue is a form of **wisdom**
* To be good is to know what is good

---

## 11. Argument from the *Meno*

Socrates’ reasoning:

1. Virtue is beneficial
2. Things like wealth and health are beneficial **only if used wisely**
3. Without wisdom, they can cause harm
4. Therefore, virtue must be **a kind of wisdom**

---

## 12. Arete and Techne

Socrates explains virtue using **crafts**:

* Shoemakers
* Metalworkers
* Craftsmen

### Techne

* Greek term meaning **practical knowledge**
* Knowing *how* to do something
* Not abstract theory, but applied understanding

> Knowledge is not just information — it is knowing how to act.

Virtue is a **special kind of techne**:

* Knowledge of how to live well
* Combined with character and judgment

---

## 13. Wrongdoing as Ignorance

From “virtue is knowledge,” Socrates concludes:

* No one knowingly does wrong
* If someone does wrong, it is because they **do not truly know the good**
* People always act according to what they believe is good

> We never intend to do what we believe is evil.

---

## 14. The Craft of Living Well

Living well is itself a **craft**:

* Just as a metalworker understands metal
* A good person understands **the self**

### Know Thyself

* Self-knowledge is essential
* One must work on the self as a craft

From the *Apology*:

> **“The unexamined life is not worth living.”**

The highest task:

* Care for the **psyche**

### Psyche

* Combination of soul and body
* The soul is imperishable
* Moral development matters more than wealth or reputation

## 15. The Trial of Socrates (Historical Context)

### Political Background

* Athens lost the **Peloponnesian War** against Sparta
* Resulted in humiliation and instability
* Civil conflict between democrats and oligarchs

### Why Socrates Was Targeted

* He publicly exposed ignorance
* He challenged tradition and authority
* He was seen as a dangerous social critic

### The Charges

* Not worshipping the gods of the state
* Corrupting the youth

Many Athenians believed:

* Questioning traditions weakened Athens
* Socrates made the city vulnerable

This led to his trial.

* Socrates defended himself, but he was judged guilty by a rather close vote.
* He was sentenced to death. He accepted the judgment and refused to flee into exile.
* He was put to death by drinking a cup of poison hemlock.

## Key Thread So Far

* Sophists: persuasion without truth
* Socrates: truth through examination
* Knowledge over opinion
* Virtue as wisdom
* Philosophy as a way of life, not a profession

> Philosophy begins when we stop asking how to win — and start asking how to live.
