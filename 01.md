# 📘 Big Ideas in Philosophy – Week 1

## What Is Philosophy? | From Mythos to Logos | Pre-Socratic Thinkers

## 1. What Is Philosophy?

### Everyday vs Academic Meaning

* In **everyday language**, “philosophy” often means:

  * A *reason* or *rationale* behind a decision

    > e.g., “What was your philosophy for choosing this program?”
* As an **academic discipline**, philosophy:

  * Is one of the **oldest disciplines in human history**
  * Originally included what we now call **science** (physics, biology, etc.)

### Philosophy as the First Academic Discipline

* Around **2500 years ago**, disciplines like physics and natural science were considered **branches of philosophy**
* Philosophy laid the **foundation for all systematic inquiry**



## 2. What Do Philosophers Study?

Philosophy investigates **general and abstract features** of the world and human life, such as:

* **Happiness** → What does it mean to live a good life?
* **Ethics (Moral Philosophy)** → What is right or wrong?
* **Political Philosophy** → What is the best form of government? Why obey authority?
* **Metaphysics** → What exists? Does God exist?
* **Philosophy of Mind** → What is consciousness? Is the mind separate from the body?
* **Epistemology** → What is knowledge? How do we know things?
* **Philosophy of Science** → What is science? What counts as scientific knowledge?

📌 Philosophy asks **fundamental questions** rather than studying specific cases.



## 3. Philosophy as Knowledge Through Arguments and Concepts (Hegel)

### Hegel’s Definition

> **Philosophy is knowledge through arguments and concepts**

This definition emphasizes two core features:

### 1️⃣ Arguments

* Philosophical claims **must be supported by reasoning**
* Simply stating an opinion is not enough
* Every claim requires a **justification**

### 2️⃣ Concepts

* Philosophy deals with **general ideas**, not specific objects

  * Not *this* human → but **human beings**
  * Not *this* action → but **justice**, **freedom**, **truth**



## 4. Why Philosophy Is Similar to Geometry (Plato)

### Plato and Geometry

* Plato founded the **Academy**
* Inscription at the Academy:

  > *“Let no one ignorant of geometry enter here.”*

### Why Geometry?

* Geometry:

  * Makes **claims** (theorems)
  * Supports them with **proofs**
* Philosophy works the same way:

  * Claim → Argument → Conclusion

📐 **Example (Geometry):**

* Claim: The perimeter of a square is 4 times one side
* Proof: All sides are equal → P = AB + AB + AB + AB = 4 × side

📐 **Philosophy follows the same structure**



## 5. Philosophy vs Theology

### Similarity

* Both discuss:

  * God
  * Creation
  * Meaning of existence

### Key Difference

| Theology                | Philosophy                |
| -- | - |
| Relies on **scripture** | Relies only on **reason** |
| Bible, Quran, Vedas     | Logical argument          |
| Faith-based             | Rational analysis         |

📌 Philosophers **cannot appeal to sacred texts** to justify claims.



## 6. Example: Philosophical Argument (Cosmological Argument)

* Whatever begins to exist has a cause
* The universe began to exist
* **Therefore, the universe has a cause**

This structure mirrors **geometric proof**:

* Premises → Logical inference → Conclusion



## 7. Birth of Philosophy: From Mythos to Logos

### Mythos

* Explanation through **mythology**
* Appeals to **supernatural forces**
* Common in ancient cultures

📖 Greek mythology (Homer & Hesiod):

* **Homer** → *Iliad*, *Odyssey*
* **Hesiod** → *Theogony*

### Polytheism

* Ancient Greece was **polytheistic**
* Multiple gods tied to natural and human phenomena:

  * **Zeus** → Thunder & lightning
  * **Poseidon** → Sea & earthquakes
  * **Ares** → War (⚠️ correction: Ares, not Eros)
  * **Eros** → Love
  * **Eris** → Strife & conflict



## 8. Limits of Mythological Explanation

* Myth answers *why* something happened:

  * “Zeus caused lightning”
* But myth:

  * **Cannot predict future events**
  * **Cannot form laws of nature**
  * Depends on divine will (anger, jealousy, mood)

📌 Knowledge requires **predictability and consistency**



## 9. Logos: Rational Explanation Based on Nature

### Logos Means:

* Explanation based on:

  * **Observation**
  * **Reason**
  * **Inherent principles within the world**

### Key Shift

| Mythos              | Logos                  |
| - | - |
| Gods explain nature | Nature explains itself |
| External causes     | Internal causes        |
| Supernatural        | Rational               |

👉 This transition marks the **birth of philosophy and science**



## 10. Pre-Socratic Philosophers

### Why “Pre-Socratic”?

* Came **before Socrates**
* Initiated the move from myth to rational explanation



## 11. Thales of Miletus (c. 624–546 BCE)

📍 From **Miletus** (modern-day Turkey)

### Central Question

> What is the **one underlying principle** behind all natural phenomena?

### Unity in Diversity

* Many different events exist
* Yet they are **interconnected**
* Therefore, there must be a **common source**

### Thales’ Answer

> **Everything comes from water**

* Water is the **archē** (ἀρχή) → primary substance
* All things are different forms of the same basic stuff

📌 Important:
The value of Thales’ work is **not** that water is correct, but that:

* He sought a **unified natural explanation**
* Without appealing to gods



## 12. Importance of Thales’ Project

* First attempt at a **Theory of Everything**
* Similar in spirit to **modern physics** (e.g., Stephen Hawking)
* Shifted explanation:

  * From divine will → natural law
* Allowed for:

  * Prediction
  * Rational understanding
  * Scientific inquiry



## 13. Anaximenes of Miletus

* Student of Thales
* Continued the same project
* Proposed:

  > **Air** is the primary substance

📌 Change of substance doesn’t matter
📌 **Method** is what matters



## 14. Anaximander of Miletus

### Major Advancement

* Asked a deeper question:

  > “What explains the primary substance itself?”

### Key Ideas

* Everything has a **beginning**
* Every state of things must be explained by a prior state
* Therefore, the ultimate source cannot be a familiar element

### His Proposal

* The **Apeiron** (ἄπειρον) → the indefinite / boundless
* A non-material, abstract principle
* First major step toward **metaphysics**

📌 Anaximander:

* Rejected myth
* Demanded rational explanation **all the way down**



## 15. Why This Matters

* Philosophy begins when humans:

  * Stop appealing to gods
  * Start trusting **reason**
* The **mythos → logos** shift:

  * Created philosophy
  * Laid foundations for science
  * Changed how humans understand the world



## ✅ Key Takeaways

* Philosophy = **reasoned inquiry**
* Philosophy works through:

  * **Arguments**
  * **Concepts**
* Birth of philosophy occurred in **archaic Greece**
* Pre-Socratics:

  * Explained the world through **nature itself**
* This transition marks the **origin of rational thought**
